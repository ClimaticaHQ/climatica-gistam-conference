Climate change has become one of the most pressing global concerns. According to the
Peoples' Climate Vote 2024~\cite{undp2024pcv}, roughly seven in ten people worldwide report
that climate change influences their major life decisions. Understanding how temperature,
precipitation or solar radiation evolve in a given region, and comparing climate across
periods or locations, can support informed decisions in agriculture, tourism, urban
planning and many other domains.

However, access to climate data is not that easy for everyone.
Climate data itself is abundant. WorldClim~\cite{worldclim}, for instance, is an open dataset
offering global gridded climate surfaces with 26 variables (including temperature,
precipitation, solar radiation and wind speed) at resolutions from 30 arc-seconds to
10 arc-minutes, covering historical, recent and future periods. However, despite the rich data 
provided by WorldClim, it is combersome to retrieve and visualize needed information. It 
requires downloading large raster files and processing them with specialised GIS software,
which demands time and expertise that most potential users lack.

Existing web tools only partially address this problem. Climate-Charts.net~\cite{climatecharts},
a representative example, relies on raw meteorological station records, leaving large regions
poorly covered and values between stations unreliable; It is also restricted to a single historical 
baseline and offers no regional analysis.

Other tools, reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:related:existing-tools}, show similar issues: narrow regional
coverage, point-based or coarse resolution, limited time periods, and static
single-location views without comparison across places or periods. 
WorldClim does not suffer from these
limitations; on the contrary provides gridded global coverage, fine spatial resolution and historical, 
recent and future periods. what it lacks is an accessible interface, to  make the data easy to use by 
everyone who needs it.

% ? proposed solution
To bridge this gap, we present \climatica{}, a web application that provides interactive
access to WorldClim data without requiring GIS expertise. Its main contributions are:
(i)~visualisation of selected climate variables for any location worldwide, chosen on an
interactive map, as Walter--Lieth climographs or bar charts;
(ii)~comparison of climate across time periods and between locations;
(iii)~regional heatmaps for analysing user-drawn areas; and
(iv)~export of results as images (PNG, SVG) or raw data (CSV, JSON) for further research.

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} introduces the WorldClim dataset
and describes existing climate visualisation tools. Section~\ref{sec:web-application} presents \climatica{},
covering its requirements, data integration, architecture and main features. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} describes
the user evaluation and its results, also covering some discussion quiestions, and section~\ref{sec:conclusions} 
concludes the paper and outlines the future work.
